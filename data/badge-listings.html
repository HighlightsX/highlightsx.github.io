Badge listings, graded

The twelve directories that carried a badge in our homepage footer, ordered best to worst. Every grade is built from values read off the live pages, last checked 24 September 2026: the rel on the link back, the robots directives, whether the listing sits in their sitemap, whether anything on their own site links to it, and how many other outbound links share the page. Nothing here is recalled.

Two have now dropped us. Wired Business and Twelve Tools, which are one operator, both pulled the link on 24 September, three days after the badges came down. Both pages still load and still describe the site; each now carries a line saying the listing was unlisted because the badge is no longer displayed. The other eight are untouched.

How the grades were set

Four measured things, in order of weight. A link nothing can reach is worth less than a link with an awkward rel, which is why discovery outranks everything under it.

1. Does it pass at all. The rel token list, the robots meta tag and the x-robots-tag header. Only nofollow, sponsored and ugc block; noopener and noreferrer pass in full. All ten live listings pass, and none carried an x-robots-tag, so this test separates nobody.
2. Can a crawler find the page. Is it in their sitemap, does anything on their own site link to it, is it canonical to itself. This is what actually spread the grades apart.
3. How much the page is diluted. Count of outbound links sharing the page: four on SpotStartups against ninety on EasyLaunch.
4. Whose voice it is. Three pairs here run identical templates, so six of the ten links come from three operators. Two listings from one operator count roughly as one mention.
The one that surprised us. Wired Business has the best rel of the twelve, no attribute at all, and still lands at C+. Their sitemap carries 112 URLs and not a single one is a listing page. Twelve Tools, its sibling, has no sitemap, does not show us on its homepage, and does not show us on the category page its own breadcrumb assigns us to. That is the D: perfect markup that nothing can reach.
Two down, eight standing. The badges came down on 21 September and the first reaction landed on 24 September, faster than the week-or-more these directories usually take. Both were the same operator, so this is one decision, not two. The remaining eight have not moved: same links, same attributes, same robots directives as on 22 September. Worth re-checking through mid-October before calling the rest safe.
